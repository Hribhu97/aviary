Flagship Species · Australia

BUDGERIGAR

Melopsittacus undulatus

Size
Approx. 18 cm in length, one of the smallest parrot species commonly kept and studied.
Lifespan
5–10 years in the wild; often longer (up to 15+ years) in well-managed captivity.
Family
Psittaculidae — the parrot family, within the broader order Psittaciformes.
Native Range
Endemic to the interior and drier regions of mainland Australia.
Habitat
Open grasslands, scrublands, and semi-arid woodland near water sources.
Movement
Highly nomadic in the wild — large flocks track rainfall and seed availability.
Diet
Primarily grass seeds in the wild; a varied diet in captivity including pellets, seed mix, and fresh greens.
Social Behaviour
Highly social and gregarious — wild flocks can number in the thousands.
Breeding
Opportunistic breeders, often timed to rainfall and food abundance rather than a fixed season.
Vocal Range
Known for chattering, warbling calls, and — in captivity — a notable capacity for mimicry.
Audio Source
Information currently unavailable — verified audio recordings pending source attribution.
Function
Contact calls help maintain cohesion within large, fast-moving wild flocks.
IUCN Status
Least Concern — wild populations remain large and widespread across Australia.
Population Trend
Considered stable; fluctuates naturally with rainfall cycles typical of arid-zone species.
Mutations
Numerous colour mutations exist in captive-bred lines (e.g. blue, albino series); verified genetic detail available on request.
